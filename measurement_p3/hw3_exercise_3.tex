\subsection{Poverty Headcount Rate at \$1/Day (237
FCFA)}\label{poverty-headcount-rate-at-1day-237-fcfa}

\textbf{Question 1:} Compute poverty headcount at 237 FCFA/day (\$1/day
PPP).

Table \ref{tbl-poor_headcount_237} shows the poverty headcount rates at
the extreme poverty threshold of 237 FCFA/dayacross regions and over
time. Nationally, the poverty headcount rate increased substantially
from 21.8\% in 1987 to 44.2\% in 1998 and 51.4\% in 2008, representing
an overall rise of 29.6 percentage points. Most of this national
deterioration occurred during the first decade (+22.4 percentage points
between 1987 and 1998) before decelerating between 1998 and 2008 (7.2
percentage points). Across regions, both the North and the South show
increases in extreme poverty over the data period, though along
different paths. In the South, the poverty headcount rate rose by 30.6
percentage points overall, jumping sharply from 20.9\% in 1987 to 45.8\%
in 1998 (24.9 percentage points) and then increasing slower to 51.5\% in
2008 (5.7 percentage points). In the North, extreme poverty rose more
steadily by 26.6 percentage points overall, from 24.4\% in 1987 to
38.9\% in 1998 (+14.5 percentage points) and 51.0\% in 2008 (+12.1
percentage points). Comparing the two regions directly, the North--South
difference is not statistically significant from zero in 1987. However,
because the South suffered a higher initial shock between 1987 and 1998,
extreme poverty became 6.9 percentage points higher in the South than in
the North in 1998. Faster poverty growth in the North between 1998 and
2008 eventually closed this gap, leading to a convergence around 51\% by
2008.

\subsection{Poverty Headcount Rate at \$2/Day (474
FCFA)}\label{poverty-headcount-rate-at-2day-474-fcfa}

\textbf{Question 2:} Compute poverty headcount at 474 FCFA/day and
compare.

Columns 1-4 of Table \ref{tbl-poor_headcount_474} show the poverty
headcount rates at the 474 FCFA/day threshold and the regional gap,
while Columns 5-8 show the difference between the two thresholds
(\({474} - {237}\)), which measures the share of the population living
in moderate poverty between 237 and 474 FCFA/day. Doubling the poverty
line raises measured poverty dramatically, revealing that a large share
of the population is clustered just above the extreme poverty line.
Nationally in 1987, the poverty headcount rate at 474 FCFA/day was
62.8\% higher than at 237 FCFA/day (21.8\%), meaning that 41.0\% of the
population lived on between 237 and 474 FCFA/day. Mirroring the \$1/day
trajectory, national poverty at 474 FCFA/day surged between 1987 and
1998 (+18.0 percentage points to 80.8\%) before slowing between 1998 and
2008 (+1.9 percentage points to 82.7\%). Over the period of the survey,
the 19.9 percentage point increase in poverty at the 474 FCFA/day
threshold reflects a 29.6 percentage point increase in extreme poverty
below 237 FCFA/day (from 21.8\% to 51.4\%) combined with a 9.7
percentage point contraction in the moderate poverty bracket (from
41.0\% to 31.3\%), indicating peole moved from moderate into extreme
poverty.

Within regions, the time and composition of this poverty increase differ
between North and South:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{North:} In 1987, 58.7\% of the North population lived below 474 FCFA/day, split between extreme poverty below 237 FCFA/day (24.4\%) and moderate poverty between 237 and 474 FCFA/day (34.3\%). Between 1987 and 1998, poverty at 474 FCFA/day jumped by 22.2 percentage points to 80.9\%, driven by both rising extreme poverty (+14.5 percentage points) and an increment of the moderate poverty bracket (+7.7 percentage points to 42.0\%). Between 1998 and 2008, the \$2/day rate increased more to 83.1\% overall since 1987, as people in the moderate poverty group fell into extreme poverty (the moderate poverty share decreased by 9.9 percentage points back to 32.1\%, while extreme poverty increased another 12.1 percentage points to 51.0\%).

\item \textbf{South:} In 1987, 64.1\% of the South population lived below 474 FCFA/day 43.1 percentage points higher than at 237 FCFA/day (20.9\%). In 1998, the South poverty rate increased sharply by 16.7 percentage points to 80.8\%, driven by a 24.9 percentage point raise in extreme poverty (from 20.9\% to 45.8\%) partially compensated by an 8.1 percentage point reduction in the moderate poverty bracket (from 43.1\% to 35.0\%). Between 1998 and 2008, poverty at 474 FCFA/day increased by 1.8 percentage points to 82.6\% overall, as extreme poverty rose another 5.7 percentage points to 51.5\% while the moderate bracket reduced more to 31.1\%.
\end{itemize}

Comparing across regions (Columns 4 and 8) highlights both regional
convergence and a shift in the distribution of poverty. In 1987, the
North actually show a statistically significant lower poverty headcount
rate at 474 FCFA/day than the South by 5.4 percentage points (58.7\%
vs.~64.1\%), because the moderate poverty bracket was 8.9 percentage
points smaller in the North than in the South (34.3\% vs.~43.1\%). In
1998, because the South show a fall into extreme poverty while the North
fell primarily into the intermediate bracket, the moderate poverty share
became 7.0 percentage points larger in the North than in the South
(42.0\% vs.~35.0\%), while total poverty at 474 FCFA/day converged
across regions. By 2008, after the North also experienced a fall into
the extreme poverty group, both the overall \$2/day poverty rate and the
moderate poverty share converged completely, with no regions gap
statistically different from zero.

\subsection{Impact of Unadjusted Spatial
Prices}\label{impact-of-unadjusted-spatial-prices}

\textbf{Question 3:} Recompute poverty assuming uniform CPI (South CPI)
and PPP = 1 across regions.

By setting the CPI to the South's CPI and PPP to 1, we can assess how
much of the measured poverty differences are due to spatial price
differences essentially ignoring the fact that the north has lower
prices than the south. Table \ref{tbl-poor_headcount_uniform} shows the
poverty headcount rates at 237 FCFA/day (columns 1-4) and their
differences when setting different CPI for the north region (columns
5-7). By definition the South estimates are unaffected by the CPI
adjustment because it is the reference region (column 7).

The results shows that at the national level, the poverty headcount rate
is sistematically higher when using the South CPI and PPP = 1, with a
difference of 3.3 percentage points in 1987, 6.9 percentage points in
1998 and 5.1 percentage points in 2008, relative to setting the proper
CPI for the north region (Question 3.a). The North-South difference is
also systematically higher because the North higher extreme poverty
rates: 38.1\% in 1987, 68.6\% in 1998, and 73.8\% in 2008 which
correspond to 13.7, 29.6, and 22.8 percentage points difference
realative to previous estimates. Overall, omitting spatial price
differences overestimates the actual poverty rates in the North, while
the South is unaffected by the CPI adjustment.
